## Experience
---
One experience I'd like to share happened when I applied for a **CCTV Technician** position.

During my interview, HR looked at my resume and asked,

> "Can you repair PCs or laptops?"

I answered honestly.

Since I had previously worked as an **Electronics Technician**, I told them that as long as the proper tools and replacement parts were available, I was confident I could diagnose and repair computers.

After getting hired, I discovered that my responsibilities weren't limited to CCTV systems. I also became a **Junior Network Administrator**, which meant handling office networking and supporting employees with their daily technical issues.

One of the tasks I ended up doing almost every day was connecting and troubleshooting office printers.

At first, I thought the job was simply connecting printers to Wi-Fi. In reality, that was only the beginning.

Sometimes the printer couldn't obtain an IP address. Other times the driver was missing, the print queue became stuck, or users suddenly couldn't print because of network configuration changes.

Another challenge was communication.

Most of the office staff were foreigners, and many primarily spoke Mandarin or Cantonese. Since I couldn't rely on asking detailed questions, I had to become comfortable troubleshooting independently. I spent many hours reading manuals, watching tutorials, searching online, and experimenting until I found the solution.

One thing I quickly realized is that experienced technicians don't memorize every printer error code.

Instead, they know **where to look**, **how to search efficiently**, and **how to eliminate possible causes one by one**.

That mindset helped me survive in the workplace much more than memorizing technical information.

> **Good technicians don't know every answer. They know how to find the correct answer efficiently.**
